## Failure analysis

**H03 (held-out) - exhibition booth, expected: submitted.** Tam found the right item, price, vendors, budget,
method and approval chain, then asked which expo, booth size and which department to file under, before
committing 45,000 SAR. Counted as a failure against the predefined expectation. It is an over-cautious
clarification rather than a wrong action, and we deliberately did **not** tune against it, to keep the held-out
score honest.

## What the ablations show

* **Rules layer off:** the LLM alone caught the planted form mistakes, but it submitted a support contract and
  licenses without the controller they belong to: a policy-breaking request reached approvers. With the rules layer
  on, policy-breaking submissions were 0 in every run. Judgment comes from the LLM; guarantees come from code.
* **Lookup tools off:** pass rate halves. In most cases the agent correctly refused to guess; in the Arabic printer
  case it guessed 4,200 SAR per printer (real: 14,500), so the request went to the Head of Finance instead of
  the CFO. Grounding in company data is what makes the numbers and the routing right.

## Cost

All development and evaluation runs so far (6 runs, 83 agent conversations) cost **US$0.30** on
`claude-haiku-5-5`: about **0.4 US cents per request** handled end to end.

## Business impact: before vs after

"Before" figures are estimates from the author's internship in a Saudi investment company's IT department
(not audited data). "After" figures are measured in this prototype on the synthetic company.

| | Before (manual, observed) | After (Tam, measured) |
|---|---|---|
| Drafting a complete, checked purchase request | Manual form, back-and-forth on wording | ~10 s per request (avg 9.5-11 s in evals) |
| Requests needing rework for vague or wrong content | ~1 in 3 | Vague text rewritten and errors flagged **before** routing; 0 policy-breaking submissions in all non-ablation runs |
| Requests re-signed after an error was found late | ~1 in 7 | Errors blocked before the first signature; text locked after signing |
| Emails / Teams messages per request | 10-50 | 0 manual: one automatic request per approver, plus reminders and escalation |
| Print / scan cycles | 4 (normal) to 12 (CFO + CEO with justification) | 0: one-click digital approvals with a hash-chained audit log |
| Cycle time | Urgent 1-5 days; large requests weeks to over a month | Limited only by approver decisions; urgent requests reminded after 6 h and escalated after 24 h |
| Audit evidence for one purchase | Searching SAP, OneDrive and email by name; audits took weeks | One-click audit pack per purchase or per year; compliance findings computed automatically |
