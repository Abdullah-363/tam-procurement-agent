# Tam: complete feature guide

Everything Tam does, by stage of a purchase request (PR). Each feature names where it lives in the code.
All company data is synthetic (fictional Raseen Holding, generated by `backend/data/make_data.py`).

Who uses Tam:

| User | Where | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Requester (department head, or staff they authorize) | **Request** view | Ask for what is needed in Arabic or English |
| Approvers (department heads, HR, accountant, Head of Finance, CFO, CEO) | **Approvals** view | Approve or reject with one click |
| Requester and approvers | **Tracker** view | See where every request is; edit text where allowed; resubmit |
| Auditor / procurement | **Audit** view | Ask questions over the records, get findings and audit packs |

---

## 1. Creating a request (Request view)

* **Plain-language requests, Arabic or English.** The requester writes the need, e.g. "10 laptops for new
  developers, urgent". Tam replies in the same language.
* **Grounded in company data** (agent tools in `backend/app/agent.py`). Tam does not guess. Before drafting it:
  * searches the **item catalog** (item codes, G/L account, last purchase price; plural and singular both match);
  * reads the **price history and registered vendors** for each item;
  * checks the **remaining budget** of the G/L account;
  * asks the policy engine for the **procurement method and approval chain**;
  * for phones, checks the **HR directory and mobile policy**.
* **Several requests in one chat.** A conversation can create more than one PR; each is tracked separately.
* **Asks instead of guessing.** If the request is too vague ("new stuff for the office"), Tam asks what is
  needed and creates nothing. Before committing large amounts with open questions, it asks to confirm.
* **New items.** Something never bought before gets a `NEW-` code and an estimated price; procurement sources it.
* **Urgent or normal.** Saying "urgent" or "asap" marks the PR Urgent, which changes follow-up timing (section 4).
* **"What Tam did" panel.** Every lookup, check and action for the latest message is shown in order, with the
  errors and warnings it found.
* **Chat memory.** The conversation survives switching views and page reloads; **Clear chat** starts over.

## 2. Checking before anyone signs (deterministic rules, `backend/app/rules.py`)

Every draft is validated by code, not by the AI. A PR with any **error** cannot be submitted; warnings are shown.

| Check | Example it catches |
|---|---|
| Required fields | Missing title, department, G/L account, items or justification |
| Known items and quantities | Item not in the catalog; quantity zero or negative |
| Price far above last purchase (>20%) | Access points at 4,600 when the last purchase was 3,400 |
| Price far below reference | 650 instead of 6,500 (likely a missing zero) |
| Dependencies | Support contract or licenses without the controller they belong to |
| Total matches the items | "Estimated value 6,500" when the items add up to 66,800 |
| G/L account matches the items | Laptops charged to the software licenses account |
| Procurement method | Low Value claimed for a 66,800 SAR request (needs RFQ) |
| False sole source | Sole source claimed when two vendors sell the item |
| Sole-source justification | Sole source without explaining why only this supplier |
| Vendor | Preferred vendor not registered, or not a seller of the item |
| Justification quality | "need laptops" flagged as too vague; Tam rewrites it |
| Budget | 195,000 requested with 181,600 left in the account |
| Mobile policy | Wrong package for the grade; interns; people who left; a second line |

When the AI is told to ignore the rules (prompt injection in the request), the rules still apply: tested in
held-out case H11.

## 3. Policy Tam applies

* **Procurement method by value:** Low Value below 20,000 SAR · RFQ with 3 quotes from 20,000 to 199,999 ·
  Competitive Bidding from 200,000 · Sole Source (no bidding, written justification required) ·
  Framework Agreement call-offs (company phones).
* **Approval chain:** department head → HR (when needed) → accountant → Head of Finance below 40,000, CFO from
  40,000, CFO and CEO from 200,000.
* **HR headcount confirmation** is added automatically for new per-user seats (licenses for new users) and for
  equipment for new hires; not for renewals of existing seats.
* **Nobody signs twice.** If the same person would appear twice (e.g. Finance's own request), the chain escalates
  to the next level instead.
* **Company phones by grade:** managers get an iPhone Pro with an unlimited plan and unlimited roaming; staff get
  an iPhone with a plan without unlimited roaming; interns are not eligible. Chain: HR → department head → IT head.
  After final approval the line is ordered under the framework agreement and recorded against the employee.
* **Policy is data, not code.** Thresholds live in the company policy; changing the Low Value limit at run time
  changes Tam's behaviour with no code change (held-out case H15).

## 4. Approvals and follow-up (Approvals and Tracker views, `backend/app/workflow.py`)

* **One-click decisions.** Choose the approver role, then **Approve**, or **Reject** with a reason.
* **Only the current approver can sign.** Signing out of order is refused.
* **Automatic reminders and escalation:**

  | Request type | Reminder | Escalation |
  |---|---|---|
  | Urgent | after 6 hours | after 24 hours |
  | Normal | after 48 hours | after 96 hours |

  Escalations go to the next level (e.g. a silent department head or HR to the CFO; the accountant to the Head of
  Finance). The top bar's **+6 hours / +1 day / +3 days** buttons move the simulated company clock to show this.
* **Tracker.** Every request with its status, who it is waiting for, the approval journey, items, justification
  and the full time-stamped audit trail. A green **Approved** stamp appears when it is fully approved.

## 5. Rejection, justification memo and resubmission

This mirrors the real rule that a rejected request needs a written justification before it goes back.

1. An approver **rejects** with a reason ("Reason for rejecting" box).
2. Tam takes ownership of the rejection automatically: it reads the PR, the reason, price history, budget and
   policy, and drafts a **justification memo** with six sections:
   1. Business need · 2. Why this solution or vendor · 3. Alternatives considered · 4. Cost and budget impact ·
   5. Risk if not approved · 6. Response to the approver's concern.
3. The requester **reviews and edits the memo** in the Tracker (pen button), and can also edit the PR's
   justification.
4. **Approve memo and resubmit** sends the PR back into the approval chain as a new round. The memo is attached
   as its own document; it is not pasted into the justification.
5. **Earlier rounds are kept:** the Tracker shows "Round 1: returned by ... with the reason", and the printable
   PR form lists every approval round.

## 6. Editing rules (who can change the text, and when)

| Who | Can edit | When |
|---|---|---|
| Requester | Justification, memo | While a draft, after a rejection, or before anyone has signed |
| Department head | Justification | While the request waits for their own signature |
| Anyone | Nothing | After the first signature: what was signed must not change silently |

Every edit is logged with who changed it and the text before the change, and the PR is re-validated.
To change a request that is already signed, an approver rejects it, which sends it back for edits.

## 7. Hand-over to procurement

When the last approver signs, Tam:
* marks the request **Sent to procurement** and commits the amount against the budget;
* notifies the Head of Procurement with the value, required method and preferred vendor (CC Head of IT);
* attaches the approved PR form with all signatures;
* for phones, sends IT the call-off order and records the new line against the employee.

**Open PR form** shows a printable purchase request form: header, items, totals, method checkboxes (including
Framework Agreement), employee details for phone requests, justification, and every approval round.

## 8. Acting on its own (scheduled scan)

**Run scheduled scan (30 days)** (or 90 days) in the Tracker runs what a nightly job would run:
* finds licenses and contracts **expiring soon** (e.g. Adobe, SAP) and opens the **renewal request** itself,
  with the renewal quote price, right method and approvers;
* finds **phone lines still billed for people who have left** and asks IT to cancel them, with the monthly saving;
* if nothing needs action, it says so without calling the AI.

## 9. Audit (Audit view)

* **Ask the records** in plain language, e.g. "Which purchases this year broke procurement policy?", "Show IT
  purchases over 40,000 SAR and who approved each", "Which phone lines are we still paying for people who left?".
  Answers come only from the purchase records and the audit log, with PR numbers as evidence.
* **Automatic compliance findings:** wrong method for the value, missing approvals (e.g. CEO), too few quotes,
  missing documents (e.g. invoice), lines billed after the employee left.
* **Audit pack:** one click for a printable pack for the whole year or a single PR (**Audit pack for this PR**).
* **Tamper-evident log:** every action is stored in a SHA-256 hash chain. **Verify audit log** checks it;
  **Simulate tampering** edits one record so you can see verification fail.

## 10. Demo controls

* **Company clock** with +6 hours, +1 day and +3 days, to show reminders, escalation and renewals.
* **Reset demo** restores the synthetic company to its starting state.
* **Light / dark mode.**
* **Model and usage** in the sidebar: which Claude model is used, runs and tokens.

## 11. Safety and limits by design

* The AI never decides budgets, thresholds or approvers by itself; it calls tools, and the rules block any
  submission that breaks policy. The ablation without the rules layer let a policy-breaking request through;
  with it, zero in every run (`backend/evals/RESULTS.md`).
* Step and token limits on every agent run (`TAM_MAX_STEPS`, `TAM_MAX_TOKENS`).
* Limits of the prototype are listed in the README: simulated integrations (no live SAP, email or Teams), one
  company, no sign-in or role permissions.
